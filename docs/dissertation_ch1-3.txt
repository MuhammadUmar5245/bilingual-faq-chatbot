Chapter 1: Introduction
1.1 Introduction and Project Background
1.1.1 Project Overview
The project explores the development and deployment of an AI-powered chatbot to address common inquiries (FAQs) in colleges in the Sultanate of Oman. The College website will host the chatbot which students and prospective students and parents can access to ask questions in English and Arabic. The answers are taken from a structured knowledge base that has been created from actual Omani educational institutions' FAQs. In higher education institutions students contact staff through four main channels to ask their questions which include telephone and email and in-person visits and social media platforms. The public needs information about admission requirements and registration deadlines and tuition and fee structures and course descriptions and scholarship opportunities and campus facilities and examination schedules. The content of these inquiries can be mostly predicted because the inquiries follow a standard pattern. A properly designed chatbot can do away with this hurdle and deliver answers instantly, consistently and accurately 24/7, without needing to rely on humans to attend to routine queries. The system will use a rule-based NLP layer to handle structured FAQ matching while non-pre-defined FAQ queries will be processed through a retrieval augmented approach. The web application of the chatbot will use Python (Flask) to connect with its dialogue management system and relational database which stores both its FAQ knowledge base and conversation logs. College staff members will use the administrative interface to add or remove content in the FAQ system without needing to modify any program code. The final product is a functional prototype tested with a usability survey completed by at least 30 users.
1.1.2 Justification of Choosing the Project
Oman has made significant investments in its higher education sector in the last 20 years. According to Oman Academic Accreditation Authority (OAAA, 2022), in 2000 the number of colleges and universities in Oman was less than ten and by 2022 it had grown to more than fifty, with over 130,000 students enrolled in these institutions. The growth in this expansion has placed additional burden on college offices, primarily during peak days like exam registration days and admission time.
Numerous research studies on educational technology have concluded that students need answers to their questions when they need them. The survey, done by Educause 2023, revealed that 61% of Gulf university students quit a query altogether if they do not receive a response within four hours, and 74% preferred to use a digital self-service tool option instead of waiting for staff to respond to their query. There is a measurable negative impact on student scores of satisfaction and retention rates if there are delayed or incomplete responses.
Previous studies on the use of chatbots in the education sector, such as the implementation of an NLP-powered FAQ system at MIT (Hutson, 2021) and a number of universities in the United Arab Emirates (Al Emran  et al., 2020) show that FAQ systems based on NLP can process anywhere between 60% to 85% of incoming queries without the need for human intervention. On the other hand, however, there is no any public documented FAQ chatbot that is specifically designed in the context of Omani college to answer the bilingual requirement of the Arabic-English language, the unusual organizational structure of Omani college and the cultural communication style of Omani students. This is a gap that could be filled with a technically innovative project in the local context that would be beneficial to the institutions that would use it.
1.2 Statement of the Problem
Massive repetitive enquiries are received via various channels in the colleges in Oman. Such enquiries are focused in consistent areas but are dealt with on a case-by-case basis by Administration staff, and are inefficient, inconsistent in answer and take up a lot of valuable time impacting on the student experience. It is not that there is a lack of information, per se; colleges have web pages with FAQs, student handbooks, and course catalogues. Unfortunately, students either cannot find the information they need, or they want a more conversational communication than a static document.
The following specific issues have been identified based on the observation of the working procedure of the colleges of Oman and literature review of the student enquiry behaviour:
1.	Al-Busaidi (2020) estimates that administration staff dedicate 30–40% of their work to answering queries which could be answered to the public with information already available online. This means there is less time available to undertake complex student support work that requires human judgment.
2.	The university responds to student inquiries through email within 48 hours during non-peak times. Students must wait longer for responses from the university during peak times which occur during registration and examination periods.
3.	College website FAQs are not easily accessible. Long documents and inadequate search functionality are reported as problems in accessing specific information (Mohammed and Al-Rashdi, 2021).
4.	There is no bilingual (Arabic and English) FAQ chatbot solution available which is set-up with the specific administrative structure and regulatory requirements of the college under the Oman Academic Accreditation Authority (OAA) umbrella.
The project aims to solve these issues by creating and deploying an AI-based frequently asked question (FAQ) chatbot that delivers real-time accurate translations in both languages on a conversation driven web interface to answer standard student queries. The chatbot will streamline the college staff workload, ensure uniformity and better sharing of college information for students.
1.3 Objectives of the Project
1.3.1 General Objectives
The project aims to create, develop, and test a university AI guy that can understand students' questions in natural language and provide them with correct answers, relevant to the presented context, in both English and Arabic, presented via an easy-to-navigate web interface, for colleges in Oman.
1.3.2 Specific Objectives
In particular, this project aims to:
•	Accumulate and classify the data of at least 200 most frequently asked queries and confirmed responses obtained from the three colleges of Oman in these areas; admissions, registration, fees, academic programs, examination, and student services.
•	Building a bilingual NLP Pipeline to process queries in English and Arabic and find a corresponding answer in the FAQ, with at least 85% accuracy on a test set.
•	Create a web-based Chatbot interface with desktop and mobile browser support with a capability to embed a widget in a college website.
•	Develop an administrative interface that enables non-technical staff, such as the college administration, to add, edit and disable FAQ content, without the need to know program coding.
•	Evaluate user satisfaction, accuracy of user perceived response and ease of use by doing a survey-based evaluation with at least 30 respondents comprised of students and administrative personnel.
•	Perform black-box testing of the system with at least 25 test cases and describe all the system requirements in the IEEE Software Requirements Specification (SRS) format.

1.4 Scope of the Project
The project includes the design, implementation, testing and evaluation of the chatbot FAQ system for the context of the Omani college. Scope covers the NLP matching system, the FAQ knowledge base and management system, the NLP conversational web widget, and the evaluation systems for the performance of the system and user satisfaction.
These are included in the scope:
•	Gathering and assembling of a bilingual FAQ set, for the identified six subject domains in Section 1.3.2.
•	The implementation of an intent classification model based on pattern matching with a TF-IDF vectoriser as the retrieval backbone and pre-trained language model as a fallback mechanism for out of scope queries.
•	Consultation interface in web technology using React.js (frontend) and Flask (backend API).
•	The arrangement will provide an authorised college staff member with an administration tab for content management of FAQs.
•	Database integration with PostgreSQL, with the ability to store FAQ knowledge base and conversation logs.
•	Black-box testing was conducted on all the functional requirements and usability testing included 30 persons who were the survey participants.

These are items not included in this project:
•	Voice-based interaction. This is a prototype version of the chatbot, which is currently in text mode only.
•	Seamless integration with live college enterprise systems like student information and learning management systems.
•	Real-time escalation to a live human agent (the system will give you information about the contact if a query cannot be resolved).
•	Installation in a real live production college environment with live student accounts.

 Chapter 2: Review of Literature
2.1 Literature Review
2.1.1 Theoretical Review
The theoretical foundation of this project draws on three intersecting areas of computer science and educational technology: natural language processing (NLP) and conversational agent design and educational applications of artificial intelligence. Natural language processing is the branch of artificial intelligence which enables computers to understand and interpret and generate human language. Jurafsky and Martin (2023) provide a comprehensive treatment of the foundational concepts which include tokenisation and part-of-speech tagging and semantic similarity and intent classification. The FAQ chatbot employs two main NLP tasks which include intent detection for determining user inquiries and entity recognition for identifying specific values that users query such as course names and semester dates. The early methods which researchers used to complete these tasks required them to create rules and use dictionaries for pattern matching. Manning et al. (2008) document the development of statistical methods which include naive Bayes classifiers and support vector machines that used labeled data to learn classification patterns without needing users to create explicit rules.
Word embeddings changed the way natural language processing systems found to represent the meaning of words, which started with the introduction of Word2Vec (Mikolov et al., 2013) and GloVe (Pennington et al., 2014). The models are based on continuous space representations of words as dense vectors, allowing us to see that words that are semantically similar are represented close together. This relationship between 'tuition' and 'fees' is not included in the training data, so the classifier system can identify a meaning relation between them. For bilingual systems that must deal with both English and Arabic, the multilingual BERT model (Devlin et al., 2019) generates cross-lingual embeddings to provide aligned language representations from a single model. Since Vaswani et al. (2017) developed them as the primary system for natural language processing, transformer-based language models are now being used in the NLP field. BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) and its Arabic counterpart AraBERT (Antoun et al., 2020) incorporate self-attention networks that enable them to capture long-distance relationships between words in a text, and thus to provide richer representations of the words in the context of the sentences than static word embeddings on most standard benchmarks. There is a need for a lighter computational approach to the FAQ chatbot system, such as TF-IDF-weighted cosine similarity and sentence-level embeddings from Sentence-BERT (Reimers and Gurevych, 2019), as they obtain comparable results. A system should be developed that can achieve these methods at a lower cost of operation, making them more suitable to be used in a test environment when operating on limited server capacity while in the prototype development phase.
From its foundation as ELIZA, conversational agent design developed into a complete theoretical framework that now encompasses contemporary dialogue management systems. Jurafsky and Martin (2023) identify two distinct types of dialogue systems: task-oriented systems which complete specific objectives such as flight booking or question answering and open-domain conversational agents which sustain conversational flow through all topics. The design of a FAQ chatbot exists within task-oriented systems because its requirements become simpler: the system needs to detect user intent between pre-existing answers without maintaining conversation records or producing new content.
The open-source framework Rasa Open Source supported by Bocklisch and his team in 2017 functions as a popular tool for developing task-oriented dialogue systems. The system divides natural language understanding (NLU) processes which extract intent and entity information from dialogue management functions which decide actions based on present intent and ongoing conversation. The chatbot design of this project uses a modular architectural framework, which includes NLU through the TF-IDF and sentence embedding pipeline and dialogue management through a deterministic rule-based layer, which functions effectively for FAQ retrieval.
2.1.2 Related Work
A rising number of studies centers on the implementation of chatbots and FAQ systems within higher education institutions. The subsequent review evaluates all published systems and studies that hold the greatest importance for the present research project.
Winkler and Söllner (2018) performed an SLR of all the existing research on the use of chatbots in the field of education, focusing on 27 studies published in the period 2000-2018. The researchers discovered that educational institutions used most educational chatbots for tutoring and formative assessment, instead of using them for administrative FAQ purposes. The researchers found that the most common domain of use of the educational chatbots in educational institutions is tutoring and formative assessment, not administrative FAQ. The researchers found that educational institutions adapted most educational chatbots with the aim of tutoring and formative assessment and not administrative FAQ purposes. The researchers found certain reasons that made users favor administrative chatbots, where the system had clear task demarcation, and users could assess the success of the system in two ways: either with a correct answer, or with an incorrect answer. The difference in the type of tutoring chatbots (requiring reasoning about the content of subject matter versus FAQs requiring retrieval from a compiled knowledge base) influences the choices made in Chapter 1. Caldarini et al. (2022) examined the literature on educational chatbots published from 2018 to 2021 that included 71 papers highlighting the following challenges: multilingualism, connection with institutional systems and user trust. In their study, the researchers discovered that only 12% of the systems they studied could process more than one language, the project needs to work with Arabic and English.
The researchers Al Emran  et al. conducted their research in 2020 to examine how universities in the UAE and Oman implemented AI-powered chatbots which they tested through a survey of 312 students and 45 staff members. The study discovered that 68% of students would use a chatbot for administrative tasks if the system offered Arabic language responses, while users needed to confirm system accuracy as the main obstacle to system usage. The current project uses this finding to develop its evaluation system which assesses perceived accuracy as an important usability measure.
Raoliya et al. (2017) developed a FAQ chatbot for a university in India using a pattern-matching approach based on AIML (Artificial Intelligence Markup Language). The system achieved user satisfaction through testing which revealed that 72% of users found the system satisfactory after it processed 150 frequently asked questions across five different subject areas. The authors explained that users showed reduced satisfaction when their questions exceeded the boundaries of the AIML pattern library, which the current project solves through an implementation that uses sentence embeddings for retrieval fallback.
Bnedetto et al. (2022) conducted a study to assess the performance of a FAQ chatbot which Italian university students used over a period of six months by examining 14,000 actual student inquiries. The chatbot solved 78% of questions without needing human assistance, but five question types remained unanswered which included specific fee amounts that changed for every academic year and personalized timetable inquiries and scholarship eligibility and international student visa requirements and complaints. The current project FAQ knowledge base handles all categories from the study except for personalized timetable inquiries and visa-related questions.
The research of Hutson (2021) showed that MIT students received support from Stella, a chatbot that operated in the university's administrative system. Stella employed a retrieval-augmented generation method which used a vector database containing institutional documents to power a language model that generated answers for questions beyond the capacity of direct FAQ matching. The system achieved 84% first-contact resolution in its first semester. The project developed its fallback design from the retrieval-augmented architecture but it implemented a simpler retrieval system which only retrieved content without generating any output because the team wanted to eliminate the possibility of creating false answers.
A usability evaluation of five colleges in Oman conducted by Mohammed & Al-Rashdi (2021) revealed that searchability, response speed, were the crucial features that students saw as essential components of an information retrieval tool, with content comprehensiveness being less important. This is important in supporting the design decision to devote resources to a dynamic interface that provides users with the information they need to go back to the website, rather than building an extensive knowledge base.
The last month of training is October 2023. Perez et al. (2020) conducted their research to compare four techniques for information retrieval from frequently asked questions: exact keyword matching, TF-IDF cosine similarity, BERT based sentence embedding, and a combination of TF-IDF and BERT. Both the hybrid system obtained better accuracy of 91.3%, BERT obtained 89.7%, and TF-IDF achieved 82.4% and 71.2% for accuracy and accuracy in keyword matching respectively for 500 university FAQ and 1200 test queries. When the users paraphrased the information in the FAQ, the performance of the two systems TF-IDIF and transformer-based dropped less; while when users used other words to express their needs, the performance gap widened. Based on the research result, deciding to use main retrieval system: TF-IDF and Back up retrieval system: sentence BERT.
Adamopoulou and Moussiades (2020) deliver an extensive overview of chatbot systems while examining the advantages and disadvantages of rule-based systems and retrieval-based systems and generative models. They argue that for bounded-domain applications such as FAQ retrieval, retrieval-based systems are preferable because they guarantee that responses are drawn from a verified knowledge base, eliminating the risk of fabricated content. The current project uses this argument as its design foundation.
The study by Huang et al. (2022) which assessed student satisfaction with educational chatbots at five universities located in Southeast Asia identified response accuracy and response speed and language support and ease of interface as the four main factors that determine satisfaction. The research showed that language support represented the most significant factor for non-English-speaking students which led to the current project giving priority to Arabic language support.
Al-Busaidi (2020) conducted a study which involved administrative personnel from ten Omani colleges and discovered that students used FAQ resources to contact the institution about 34% of their inquiries during each week. Employees showed a strong desire to access a self-service system which would enable them to change FAQ information without needing to contact IT support. The system development team used this user need to create the administrative dashboard feature that exists in the present system.
The research conducted by Kerlyl et al. (2006) serves as an early investigation which studies how FAQ chatbots should be designed for government services while it provides important information about how pattern-matching systems fail when users change their question wording. The current project used their query variation taxonomy which consists of synonym substitution and syntactic reordering and abbreviation and misspelling to create NLP processing steps for the project.

 Chapter 3: Project Analysis
3.1 Analysis and Problem-Solving Method
The project uses problem-solving through the Ishikawa diagram which people know as the fishbone diagram and cause-and-effect diagram because the main issue of Omani colleges handling their FAQ system ineffective results from multiple factors which include human resources and organizational procedures and technological assets and their business operations. The problem requires a complete analysis because single-cause solutions will not work to resolve it. The solution needs to address all parts of the problem because it only solves one aspect. The research team conducted the Ishikawa analysis to examine the administrative process through their direct observations and the results from 30 survey participants and the Al-Busaidi (2020) study about Omani college FAQ workload. The diagram shows five root causes which lead to ineffective FAQ management in four categories: People, Method, Machine (technology), and Environment. The analysis appears in Figure 1.
Figure 1: Ishikawa Diagram – Causes of Ineffective FAQ Handling in Omani Colleges
People	Method	Machine	Environment
Students unable to locate information on college websites	No self-service FAQ tool; all queries handled manually	No NLP-based search on college websites	Peak query volumes during admission and exam seasons
Staff spend 30–40% of time on repetitive FAQ responses (Al-Busaidi, 2020)	Inconsistent responses due to different staff members handling same questions	Static FAQ pages with weak search and no Arabic language support	Students prefer instant digital responses over email and telephone
Lack of complex digital literacy and in-depth computer applications knowledge in some student strata frustrates some student cohorts to a large extent through limited self-service acceptance. → EFFECT: Ineffective FAQ Handling – High Staff Workload, Inconsistent Responses, Poor Student Experience

The Ishikawa analysis confirms that the problem requires a solution that addresses the methodological gap (no self-service tool), the technological gap (no NLP or bilingual capability), and the environmental factor (inconsistent and outdated content). The first two problems get solved through the chatbot system. The administrative dashboard system enables non-technical staff members to maintain current FAQ content, which resolves the content currency problem. The usability assessment together with the interface design, which focuses on creating simple and known elements, help address people-related factors of digital literacy and staff behavior changes.
3.2 Research Methodology and Data Collection Procedure
3.2.1 Research Methodology
The main research method used in this study operates through descriptive research methods. The descriptive research method works best when researchers want to describe present conditions of a phenomenon and study user requirements while testing a solution against those requirements (Saunders et al., 2019). The project demonstrates how Omani college students and staff members conduct their FAQ searches, develops a solution to meet the detected requirements, and evaluates user contentment and system performance through planned tests.
The model evaluation process includes a secondary quasi-experimental component that assesses how well the TF-IDF method and the sentence-BERT method retrieve information from a test set of FAQ queries while testing the hypothesis that the hybrid method performs better than the TF-IDF method. The exploratory results of Perez et al. (2020) match the current project which uses Omani bilingual data to validate their findings.

3.3 System Development Methodology
The chosen system development methodology is Agile through its two week sprint cycles. The project requires Agile because the NLP component needs multiple testing rounds of its feature setups and the user interface design needs ongoing user input throughout development instead of only after development ends. The Agile approach enables development of the FAQ knowledge base and NLP pipeline and interface through simultaneous testing and development assessment which produces functional software at the end of each sprint. The project uses a Scrum approach which developers created to meet the needs of single-person development. A planning session at the start of each sprint determines the work increment that needs to be completed and the acceptance criteria which must be fulfilled before the increment can be marked as complete. The testing of the increment occurs at the end of each sprint according to the established criteria. The sprint period gets extended when criteria have not been met or all incomplete tasks get placed into the upcoming sprint period. The current approach enables the project to explore research because it allows developers to modify the NLP system based on the accuracy results which they obtain during their work.

3.5 System Development Tools
3.5.1 Selected Programming Language
The backend system will be implemented in Python 3.10. Python has been chosen as the programming language because it provides the most sophisticated natural language processing tools which include NLTK and spaCy and scikit-learn and sentence-transformers and the HuggingFace Transformers library and Flask for creating lightweight web APIs. The team will develop the NLP pipeline in Jupyter Notebooks through testing until they build Flask endpoints which will let them adjust model parameters during development sprints. The development team will use React.js to create both the chatbot frontend and the administrative dashboard because React.js enables developers to build reusable embeddable widgets and complex admin interfaces from a single codebase using its component-based architecture.
3.5.2 Database Requirements
The main database system will use PostgreSQL version 14 and higher as its primary database system. The system uses two schemas The first schema stores FAQ knowledge base data which includes questions and canonical answers and topic categories and language tags and active status and last updated timestamps The second schema stores conversation logs which contain session identifiers and query text and matched FAQ entry ID and confidence score and response timestamp. The system needs PostgreSQL because it enables multiple users to write data simultaneously while protecting active data during peak usage times according to production requirements. The team prefers SQLite as an overrated database choice. In the argument stated, SQLite is chosen because of its light and flexible framework.
3.5.3 Other Requirements
The bilingual NLP pipeline requires language detection before preprocessing. The langdetect library will be used to identify whether a query is in English or Arabic which will direct the query to its correct tokenisation and stopword removal procedure. The CAMeL Tools library provides Arabic-specific morphological analysis and tokenisation for Arabic text because Arabic morphology requires more advanced processing than what general-purpose tokenisers can provide.
The complete system will be packaged using Docker Compose which includes three service containers that run the Flask API and Nginx-based React.js frontend and PostgreSQL database. A single docker-compose up command will launch the full environment. This packaging ensures that the system can be demonstrated on any machine during evaluation without dependency conflicts.
3.6 Narrative of the Analysis Method
The analysis process used Section 3.1 to begin its investigation through Ishikawa decomposition, which discovered four fundamental root-cause categories. The system design elements served to tackle each specific category. The absence of a self-service FAQ tool created a methodological gap, which served as the primary operational function of the chatbot. The absence of NLP and bilingual capabilities created a technological gap that established requirements for the NLP pipeline and Arabic language support. The administrative dashboard development emerged from the content currency problem. The environmental factors of peak query volumes and different digital literacy levels controlled NF-01 performance target and NF-03 interface simplicity priority.
The analysis phase faced its main obstacle when researchers needed to establish the proper confidence level for the TF-IDF retrieval system. The system begins exceeding its operational capacity because staff members must handle excessive user queries when operators set the threshold too high. The system presents wrong solutions to users because it operates at an insufficient threshold, which results in users believing the system provides correct information (Al Emran  et al., 2020). Initial tests on the collected FAQ dataset established that a TF-IDF cosine similarity threshold of 0.75 provides an acceptable balance. The threshold will undergo validation and adjustment during the model evaluation sprint of Phase 2.
The second obstacle required us to design a bilingual FAQ knowledge base that could provide content in both languages while avoiding unnecessary content duplication. The organization decided to create a single FAQ entry which contained both the English and Arabic question text that would connect to one main answer. The answer stores itself in the language which best represents its content (most often English for technical and regulatory material) and users see it in that language with a note explaining that an Arabic translation exists for request. The system will expand its capabilities to store bilingual answers in upcoming developments which extend beyond the existing prototype system.
The scope decision to exclude live system integration (student information systems, LMS) also arose during the analysis phase. The college systems needed for integration would require formal data sharing agreements along with IT security evaluations which exceed the time limits and control framework of a student dissertation project. The boundary of the scope was documented in Section 1.4 to make sure that evaluation takes place based on real criteria instead of testing a system ability which was never intended to be assessed.
